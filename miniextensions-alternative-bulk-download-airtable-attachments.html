miniExtensions vs BaseDrop for bulk-downloading Airtable attachments

miniExtensions' Bulk Download Attachments and BaseDrop solve the same problem — getting every attachment out of Airtable as real files — in two different shapes: a cloud platform with a monthly subscription, and a Chrome extension with a one-time price. This page compares them on the facts, taken from miniExtensions' own product, documentation and pricing pages and from BaseDrop's documentation (checked October 2026). There is no single winner; there are two trade-offs.

What the miniExtensions tool does

In miniExtensions' own description, the tool downloads attachments from one or more attachment fields as a ZIP archive, over all records or a view you select. You can keep original filenames or name the copies from a field value, and place files flat, in a folder per record, or grouped by a category field. It can also generate shareable download links — for a whole collection or a single record, via a formula in a button field — with optional password protection. Their page is explicit about scope: it "exports attachments, not the structure, relationships, and configuration of the entire Airtable base".

Where miniExtensions is the better fit

If you need to give other people downloads — a client pulling their own files through a password-protected link, or a button inside Airtable that downloads one record's files — that is miniExtensions' home turf, and BaseDrop does not do it. The same goes if you already live in their ecosystem: if forms or portals from the same subscription are part of your stack, the marginal cost of their downloader is effectively zero, and one vendor is simpler than two.

Where BaseDrop is the better fit

If the job is a one-off or occasional export — a migration, a handover, a periodic archive — a subscription you keep paying between exports is the expensive way to do it; community threads regularly balk at exactly this, with one user calling a "$50/month" class of subscription "steep" for a single download utility. BaseDrop's free tier covers small exports outright, Pro costs less than one month of the subscription, and because everything runs locally in your browser tab, bases you would rather not connect to a third-party cloud never leave your machine. Two tools, two trade-offs: a platform you rent, with sharing built in — or a tool you own, that keeps the files on your side of the browser. Pick based on whether "download" in your workflow means distribute or extract.
